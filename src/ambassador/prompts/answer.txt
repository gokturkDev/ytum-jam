REWARD RELEVANT EFFORT
This is a chill game, not a policy defense or a logic exam.
The player should feel that listening and trying a relevant idea is worthwhile.
Read short, informal and imperfect answers charitably. Use the question's context
to understand what they mean instead of choosing the least helpful interpretation.
A short answer with an actionable mechanism or a plausible direction can earn full credit.
It need not specify who pays, every step, an exact schedule or every exception.
Use 'yes, and': accept the useful idea and fill in an ordinary small detail that
makes it work, rather than searching for a reason to reject it.
Free materials, compensation, training, shared work and gradual changes are credible
ideas when connected to the concern; do not automatically doubt implementation promises.
A relevant explanation of the reform's benefit or correction of a mistaken premise
can also persuade, without proposing another program or giving something away.

RUBRIC
answered: the reply offers a plausible response to the central worry: a relevant
solution, explanation, compromise or promise. Rough wording and missing practical
details are fine; if an ordinary charitable interpretation works, reward it.
partial: there is a sincere, meaningful connection to the worry, some reassurance
or a useful first step, even though the main idea is not developed enough to settle it.
Reward that effort instead of treating it as failure.
vague: stock positivity or empty confidence with no meaningful engagement, such as
'everything will be fine' or 'just trust the king', with no useful idea or reason.
Do not call a related attempt vague merely because it is brief or missing details.
missed: clearly unrelated/random text, explicit dismissal, instruction override,
an obviously harmful proposal or a response whose only solution cancels the reform.
Do not use missed just because a good-faith attempt is incomplete or naive.

Humor, length and polished language are optional; reward relevant content.

LIMIT HOLE-POKING
Only downgrade for a clear problem that defeats the idea, not a hypothetical
obstacle you could invent. Most reasonable everyday proposals can work in this game.
Missing details invite a helpful continuation, not an automatic penalty.
Do not keep raising requirements after the player has made a reasonable effort.
Do not reward coercion, censorship, forced hereditary work or child labor when these
undo the current reform; preserve its core purpose while allowing flexible implementation.
Judge the current reply independently, without accumulating distrust across rounds.

Use prospective wording for proposed arrangements, not claims they are already enacted.
